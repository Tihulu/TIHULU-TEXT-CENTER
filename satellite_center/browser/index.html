One click predicts and arms real ISS/Meteor passes; later analyze actual recordings and open verified extraction results. Browser auto capture requires previously granted RTL-SDR access and a visible, awake tab. An optional native companion can record while the browser is closed.

Capture feasibility: 1.024 MS/s unsigned IQ needs about 2.048 MB per second (60 seconds ≈ 123 MB). This browser keeps at most 128 MiB in memory; full Meteor LRPT weather images generally need longer native disk recordings with free space and SatDump. Complete coverage is never a guarantee of a complete transmitted dataset.

Browser capture limits: 60 s / 128 MiB per pass, potentially insufficient for an entire Meteor LRPT Earth image. The native service can schedule longer recordings, but SatDump/APRS decoders must actually be installed. No device is claimed until authorized, and images/messages are never inferred from a TLE pass alone.

Quick analysis samples a small number of windows. Full analysis reads every complete FFT window in small chunks without loading the entire recording; its waterfall compresses time into bounded bins. Both modes report uncalibrated digital quantities, not dBm. Candidate peaks can be noise, local electronics or RF energy; no orbit-based identification or distance estimate is made. ◉ Radio image reception

ISS SSTV is intermittent. The current ARISS event's frequency/mode takes precedence over generic presets.

ISS Robot36: record demodulated narrowband FM audio WAV, then decode it here. Raw CU8 is not WAV. Meteor LRPT: record raw CU8 during a pass and decode with SatDump (available through the optional desktop companion). A standard browser spectrum is not an LRPT decoder.

Direct browser RF decoding is experimental. For Robot36, record at least 36–40 seconds of ISS SSTV at the frequency announced by ARISS, with suitable antenna, during an actual SSTV event. 1.024 or 2.048 MS/s CU8 only. This will not decode Meteor LRPT, other SSTV modes, or random energy peaks.

Local images & capture attribution

New recording filenames use TIHULU_SATELLITE-OR-UNATTRIBUTED_YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ_frequencyHz_IQ.cu8. TLE association means candidate target, not a verified transmitter. Decoded or imported photographs receive matching satellite/date UTC names; provenance remains visible in the gallery.

Decoder availability and truthful status

Browser-only v0.3 provides genuine USB IQ collection and geometric pass estimates; experimental in-browser Robot36 decoding is available for compatible WAV/CU8 recordings; in-browser APRS/Meteor LRPT decoding is not implemented. Use SatDump or another decoder with the saved CU8 file where legal. Iridium remains RF-energy research only; no private communications decoding.

What requires more than a browser?

Windows may require WinUSB driver setup; Linux may require releasing the kernel DVB driver or adjusting permissions. To record a pass while the browser is closed, the optional local companion must remain running. The companion still supports offline SatDump and multimon-ng where installed.
